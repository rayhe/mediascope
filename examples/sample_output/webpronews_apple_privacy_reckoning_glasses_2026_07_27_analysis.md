## 1. Significance for Wearables Narrative Analysis

This article represents a critical **narrative phase transition**: the moment the smart glasses privacy backlash against Meta becomes so entrenched that it *literally delays* a competitor's product timeline. The story isn't about Meta directly — it's about Meta's privacy reputation becoming collateral damage that contaminates the entire product category.

This is the first WebProNews article analyzed in the MediaScope corpus, adding a mid-tier tech aggregator outlet to coverage.

### Why This Article Matters
1. **Category Contamination evidence:** Apple explicitly delays its smart glasses by ~1 year because Meta's glasses have "poisoned the well" for the entire category
2. **Marketing-as-weapon signal:** Apple marketing teams plan to "hammer" privacy contrast with Meta — the backlash is being instrumentalized by a competitor
3. **Single-source amplification:** The entire article derives from one Bloomberg newsletter, filtered through MacRumors, Slashdot, Neowin, Digital Trends — a textbook amplification chain
4. **Competitive positioning with moral dimension:** Apple isn't just positioning on features — it's positioning on *moral superiority* ("the responsible choice")

### 2.2 Category Contamination — NEW DEVICE (#113)
**Proposed new framing device discovered in this analysis.**

The article reveals a distinct editorial technique not fully captured by existing devices: one company's reputational damage **contaminates an entire product category**, forcing competitors to spend time/money distancing themselves.

| Instance | Quote | Mechanism |
|----------|-------|-----------|
| 1 | "Meta's reputation directly influences Apple's timeline" | Competitor timeline delayed by target's reputation |
| 2 | "Engineers don't want to ship a device that invites the same backlash" | Engineers pre-emptively avoiding category stigma |
| 3 | "How do you put cameras on someone's face without sparking outrage?" | Category-level question, not company-specific |
| 4 | "Marketing teams plan to... contrast the glasses with Meta's Ray-Ban smart glasses" | Marketing strategy built around category decontamination |

**Distinct from:**
- `competitive_positioning` (#34): company-to-company comparison. Category contamination is about one company damaging the *category itself*
- `competitive_displacement` (#36): filling a vacuum. This is about inheriting stigma, not filling a gap
- `glasshole_revival` (#107): reviving a pejorative. Category contamination is the structural consequence of that stigma
- `walking_camera` (#108): dehumanizing wearers. Category contamination frames the category as toxic regardless of company

**Proposed patterns:**
1. "[Company A]'s reputation/privacy baggage directly influences [Company B]'s timeline/strategy"
2. "don't want to ship/launch a device that invites the same backlash/criticism"
3. "How do you [enter product category] without [negative consequence]?" — question framing the category itself as the problem
4. "poisoned the well" / "poisoning the well" for [product category]

- Phase 2: Pattern recognition (multiple articles identifying recurring issues)
